3. Three outputs, one choice

What does “training” do?

The model reads each circle’s red, green, and blue amounts (RGB). Training calculates an average RGB color for each label. A new circle is compared with all three averages; the closest average wins.

The three average colors will appear here after training.

Beyond “Dark” vs. “Light”

Dark vs. Light has two possible labels: a binary classifier. This model has three: a multiclass classifier. The RGB input still has three numbers; the number of output labels is a separate choice.

Try blue or purple. There is no “Blue,” “Purple,” or “Unknown” output. The model must choose Red, Yellow, or Green—even if none is a good description. A high score can still be misleading!

A peek inside the score

This is a nearest-centroid classifier. Training learns three average RGB vectors. For prediction, RGB values are divided by 255, and we calculate squared distance to each average. Each match strength is exp(−distance / 0.15); dividing by the sum gives the three scores. The value 0.15 controls how sharp the scores look and is fixed for this demo.

We have not measured accuracy on a separate test set. Trying the training colors again checks that the demo works; it does not prove that the model understands every shade.

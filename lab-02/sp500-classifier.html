An inspectable classification experiment

Where will we be at 10:30?

Predict whether the S&P 500 at 10:30 a.m. Eastern will be above or below the previous trading day's close. All inputs must be available by 9:25 a.m. Eastern.

1. Set the morning's inputs

Returns use percentage points: enter 0.5 for a 0.5% rise.

Futures return: change from the previous session's 4:00 p.m. ET quote to today's 9:25 a.m. ET quote, using the same contract.

2. Inspect the prediction

The class boundary is 50%. Model probabilities are estimates, not calibrated confidence guarantees.

3. See what the model learned

Logistic regression adds an intercept and one contribution per input, then converts the total score into a probability. Positive contributions push toward “above”; negative ones toward “below.”

*Inputs are standardized using training data only. Contribution = learned weight × standardized input.

Train on the first 70% of examples in chronological order; test on the remaining 30%. Model settings are fixed, with no tuning on the test set.

5. Bring historical data

The initial examples are invented. To run a historical experiment, paste numeric CSV with this exact header. Each row must represent one trading day, with dates in YYYY-MM-DD order. Prices must be S&P 500 index levels, not a mix of index and ETF prices.

date,overnight_pct,previous_return_pct,vix_previous,previous_close,price_1030

At least 40 non-tied examples are required, with both labels in the training segment. Exact price ties are excluded. Use correctly timestamped intraday data; a daily closing-price dataset cannot supply the 10:30 outcome. Do not include future information in the three inputs.

Direction is not a trading rule

An index that opens 1% above yesterday's close and drops to 0.5% above it still receives the label “above.” Buying at the open in that example loses money. This page does not issue a trade signal or estimate trading profitability.

Before evaluating a trade-or-skip strategy, you would need separate entry and exit prices, position rules, transaction costs, and an untouched evaluation period. Imported data are supplied by you; this page checks their structure, not their provenance or timestamp correctness.

Runs locally in your browser. No API keys, network requests, or uploaded data. Project notes.
